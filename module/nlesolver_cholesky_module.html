Sparse direct minimum-norm linear solver, using a Cholesky factorization of (no external dependencies).

Computes the minimum-norm solution of the compatible system , where A is an m x n sparse matrix (normally n >= m) in coordinate (triplet) format:

With damp , this is the solution of the regularized problem subject to (useful if A is rank deficient).

Method

  1. Analysis (done once for a sparsity pattern, see analyze): the graph of is formed, and its rows are reordered with the reverse Cuthill-McKee (RCM) algorithm to reduce the envelope (profile) of the matrix. The envelope storage of the Cholesky factor is then allocated. Since all fill-in of a Cholesky factorization is confined to the envelope, no further symbolic work is needed.
  2. Factorization (each time the values of A change, see factor): is formed directly in the envelope storage from the sparse rows of A (the dense matrix is never formed), and factored as using a row-oriented envelope Cholesky factorization.
  3. Solve (see solve): triangular solves with L, followed by x = A^T y. Since forming squares the condition number, a few steps of iterative refinement are performed using the residual of the original system. Each correction stays in the range of , so the minimum-norm property is kept.

The cost of the factorization is , where b is the (average) bandwidth of the reordered . This is very efficient for banded or "mostly banded" Jacobians (even if the rows are not numbered in banded order). For general sparse matrices with large fill-in (e.g., 2D/3D meshes), a nested-dissection based solver (such as qr_mumps) will be faster.

References

  • A. George and J. W. H. Liu, "Computer Solution of Large Sparse Positive Definite Systems", Prentice-Hall, 1981.
  • E. Cuthill and J. McKee, "Reducing the bandwidth of sparse symmetric matrices", Proc. 24th ACM National Conference, 1969.
  • A. Bjorck, "Numerical Methods for Least Squares Problems", SIAM, 1996.
